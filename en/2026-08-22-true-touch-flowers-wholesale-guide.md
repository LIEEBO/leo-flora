# True Touch Flowers Wholesale: A Practical Guide for Wedding Planners and DIY Brides

Planning a wedding is a beautiful journey, but the costs can add up fast. If you're dreaming of lush, realistic blooms without the hefty price tag of fresh flowers, True Touch artificial flowers might be your perfect solution. For DIY brides and wedding planners, understanding how to source these high-quality faux florals wholesale can unlock a world of **affordable wedding flowers** that look anything but cheap.

This guide breaks down everything you need to know about buying True Touch flowers wholesale, from verifying materials to navigating MOQs, so you can create stunning decor with confidence.

## What Exactly Are "True Touch" Flowers?

In the wholesale world, "True Touch" is less of a specific brand and more of a category descriptor. It refers to artificial flowers made with soft-touch materials like latex, PU (polyurethane), coated fabric, or silicone blends. These materials mimic the feel of real petals far better than standard silk or polyester.

When you see terms like "Real Touch," "Natural Touch," or "Soft Touch," treat them as naming variations. The key is not the name but the material. Before placing any large order, always request a physical sample. This ensures the petals have that supple, realistic feel you're after and not just a standard silk finish.

## Why Choose Wholesale Faux Florals for Your Wedding?

Choosing wholesale artificial flowers is a smart move for several reasons. First, the cost savings are significant. You can get hundreds of stems for the price of a few dozen fresh ones, making them ideal for large installations. Second, they are durable. You can assemble your **wedding flower wall backdrop** days or even weeks in advance without worrying about wilting.

Finally, they are reusable. After the big day, you can repurpose the florals for bridal showers, baby showers, or even sell them to another bride. This sustainability factor is a huge win for budget-conscious planners.

## Key Buying Terms for Wholesale Orders

If you're sourcing from a factory or supplier, especially overseas, you'll need to understand the standard trade terms. Here’s a quick snapshot of what to expect:

- **MOQ (Minimum Order Quantity):** Usually around 500-576 pieces per style. This is a substantial commitment, so it's best for weddings with large decor needs or planners stocking up for multiple events.
- **Sample Timing:** Expect to receive samples in about 15 days. This is your chance to check the material, color, and hand-feel.
- **Bulk Production:** After sample approval, bulk production typically takes 60-90 days. Plan your timeline accordingly.
- **Trade Terms:** Most China-based exports are quoted as **FOB Xiamen**. This means the price includes getting the goods to the port, but you handle shipping and insurance.

## Practical Tips for DIY Brides and Planners

### 1. Mix and Match for Realism

To create a natural look, don't rely on just one type of flower. Mix roses with hydrangeas, add greenery, and vary the stem heights. This layering technique makes your **wedding venue decoration ideas** look professional and lush, even when using faux stems.

### 2. Invest in a Good Flower Wall

A **wedding flower wall backdrop** is a showstopper. With wholesale prices, you can afford to cover a large frame with blooms. Use a mix of large statement flowers and small fillers to create depth. This backdrop serves double duty as a photo booth and a ceremony altar, saving you money on two separate decor pieces.

### 3. Always Order Samples First

Never skip the sample step. Colors can look different on a screen, and the texture of latex versus PU can vary. Order samples of every flower type and color you're considering. This small investment prevents costly mistakes on your final order.

### 4. Plan for Packing and Recovery

Ask your supplier about export packing. Flowers with soft-touch petals can get crushed in transit. Ensure they use sturdy cartons with proper internal support. When your order arrives, unpack immediately and steam or fluff the petals to help them recover their shape.
