## Built-In Artificial Planters: How a Bistro Transformation Inspires Wedding Decor

When Westside HQ Club refurbished its bistro, the design team at BSPN Architecture didn't just want a few potted plants. They envisioned greenery that worked architecturally — integrated into banquette seating, defining zones, and creating a lush, layered botanical atmosphere. The result? A stunning, maintenance-free installation that has plenty of lessons for DIY brides and wedding planners looking to create immersive floral environments.

In this article, we'll explore how the project came together and share practical tips for using **wholesale silk flowers**, **affordable wedding flowers**, and **wedding flower wall backdrops** to achieve a similar high-impact look at your own venue.

## The Vision: Greenery That Works Architecturally

The brief for Westside HQ Club's bistro was clear: the artificial planting had to be more than decoration. It needed to define spaces, soften hard edges, and bring a dense, garden-like feel to the bistro floor. Working closely with BSPN Architecture, the design team planned custom banquette seating planters that would run along long banquette runs, curved corner triangles, and smaller intimate booths.

The challenge was density and variety. With multiple planter zones, the greenery needed to feel abundant at every scale while remaining completely maintenance-free in a high-use club environment. The solution? A fully specified planting scheme using high-quality artificial plants and flowers, supplied by Evergreen Walls, that would look fresh year-round without any watering or pruning.

## The Process: Layering Species for Maximum Impact

The supply scope covered four distinct planter configurations, each tailored to its footprint and visual role.

- **Long banquette runs:** These required the most species variety. A rich mix of Anthurium clarinervium, calathea, and spathiphyllum provided bold upright form and varied leaf texture. Calathea burgundy, philodendron, and leather fern bushes built out the mid and lower layers. Grass bush, zebra plant, and syngonium hanging bush added movement and trailing interest along the edges — spilling naturally over the joinery and reinforcing the garden-like density.

- **Banquette triangle planters:** These anchored the curved corner booths with a more dramatic vertical composition. Bird of paradise plants at 152cm and 183cm provided strong upright structure rising above the seatback line, underplanted with spathiphyllum, calathea bush, and spider plant to fill out the base and create a complete, tiered arrangement in a compact footprint.

Across all zones, custom pot/planter filler and coconut husk were used to bed each arrangement, ensuring a natural, finished appearance at soil level.

## The Result: A Lush, Layered Botanical Backdrop

The completed planter installations give Westside HQ Club a genuine sense of enclosure and character. Greenery lines the banquette seating throughout the space — framing booths, dividing zones, and creating a layered botanical backdrop that reads as lush and considered from every angle. The variety of species across the club ensures no two sections feel identical, while the consistent palette ties the whole floor together. The corner triangle arrangements deliver a striking focal point in each curved booth, with the bird of paradise plants adding dramatic height and a touch of the tropics.

## Practical Tips for Weddings and Events

Inspired by this project? Here's how to adapt the ideas for your wedding or event using artificial flowers and greenery:

1. **Plan for density and variety.** Just like the bistro, your floral installations should feel abundant. Mix different textures, heights, and colors to create depth. Use **wholesale silk flowers** to affordably build volume without blowing your budget.

2. **Integrate greenery into furniture.** Consider built-in planters in seating, bars, or archways. This creates a seamless look and saves floor space. For a dramatic focal point, a **wedding flower wall backdrop** can transform any plain wall into a photo-worthy feature.

3. **Choose the right species.** For a natural look, combine upright plants (like bird of paradise or anthurium) with trailing varieties (like syngonium or grass bush). This mimics how plants grow in nature and adds movement.

4. **Don't forget the base.** Use pot fillers like coconut husk or moss to cover the mechanics and give a finished, soil-like appearance. This small detail makes a big difference in realism.
