Hydrangeas are a beloved choice for weddings, often featured in wedding flower wall backdrops and centerpieces. Their lush, romantic blooms add a touch of elegance to any celebration. But what do you do with your hydrangeas after they finish flowering? Proper care is essential to ensure a bountiful display next year, especially if you're planning to use them in your own wedding or for a client's big day. In this guide, we'll walk you through the best practices for post-bloom hydrangea care, so you can enjoy these beautiful flowers season after season.

## Understanding Old-Wood vs. New-Wood Hydrangeas

The first step in hydrangea care is knowing what type you have. Hydrangeas fall into two main categories based on where they form their flower buds: old wood (stems from the previous year) or new wood (stems from the current year). This distinction is crucial because it determines when and how you should prune.

**Old-wood hydrangeas** include popular varieties like bigleaf (Hydrangea macrophylla), oakleaf (Hydrangea quercifolia), and mountain (Hydrangea serrata). These plants begin forming next year's flower buds in late summer, often as early as August. If you prune them too late, you risk cutting off those developing buds and sacrificing next year's blooms.

**New-wood hydrangeas**, such as smooth (Hydrangea arborescens) and panicle (Hydrangea paniculata), bloom on stems that grow in the current season. These can be pruned in late winter or early spring without affecting the flower display.

## When to Prune Old-Wood Hydrangeas

For old-wood hydrangeas, timing is everything. The safest window to prune is **immediately after flowering**—usually in late spring or early summer. This gives the plant ample time to develop new growth and set buds for the following year.

If you missed that window and it's now late summer or fall, the best approach is to be conservative. Prune only dead or damaged branches, and avoid heavy shaping. Removing healthy stems at this point could eliminate the buds that would become next year's gorgeous blooms.

## Tips for Protecting Next Year's Blooms

Beyond pruning, there are other steps you can take to ensure your hydrangeas thrive and produce plenty of flowers for your wedding decor.

- **Water deeply and regularly**, especially during dry spells. Hydrangeas prefer consistently moist soil.
- **Apply a layer of mulch** around the base to retain moisture and regulate soil temperature.
- **Feed with a balanced fertilizer** in early spring, but avoid high-nitrogen formulas that promote leaf growth over flowers.
- **Protect from harsh winter winds** by wrapping the plant in burlap or creating a simple windbreak. This is especially important for old-wood varieties in colder climates.
- **Consider deadheading** spent blooms to encourage a tidy appearance, but stop by August to avoid disturbing bud development.

## Using Hydrangeas in Wedding Decor

Whether you're growing hydrangeas for your own wedding or sourcing them for clients, these flowers are incredibly versatile. They work beautifully in bridal bouquets, table centerpieces, and even as a stunning wedding flower wall backdrop. Their large, clustered blooms create a full, luxurious look with minimal effort.

For brides on a budget, hydrangeas are a fantastic choice. They are among the most affordable wedding flowers, offering a high-impact visual at a reasonable cost. And if you're planning far in advance, consider incorporating silk flowers wedding decor into your design. High-quality faux hydrangeas can be used in backdrops and installations, while fresh ones can be reserved for bouquets and close-up arrangements. This hybrid approach allows you to enjoy the beauty of hydrangeas without breaking the bank.

## Final Thoughts

Caring for hydrangeas after flowering is simple once you understand their growth cycle. By pruning at the right time and providing proper care, you'll be rewarded with a profusion of blooms that can elevate any wedding setting. Whether you're dreaming of a romantic arch adorned with hydrangeas or a delicate centerpiece, these flowers are sure to impress. And if you're ever in doubt, remember that a little less pruning is always safer than too much. Happy gardening, and may your wedding flowers be as beautiful as you envision!
