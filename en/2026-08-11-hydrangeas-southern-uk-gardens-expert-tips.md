# Can Hydrangeas Thrive in Southern UK Gardens? Expert Tips

As the UK experiences hotter summers, the Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) is considering moving its beloved hydrangea collections from Wisley to its northern gardens. This news has left many southern gardeners—and wedding planners who adore these blooms—wondering if hydrangeas can still flourish in the south. The good news? Yes, they can—if you know how to care for them.

## Why Hydrangeas Struggle in Heat

Hydrangeas are naturally woodland plants, thriving in light shade and moist, well-draining soil. During heatwaves, their large leaves lose water quickly, and sandy soils like those at RHS Wisley dry out fast. According to RHS chief horticultural advisor Guy Barter, the move is about reducing the need for constant watering and care. But for southern gardeners, this doesn't mean giving up on hydrangeas entirely.

## Expert Tips for Growing Hydrangeas in the South

### 1. Choose the Right Location

Plant hydrangeas in north-facing spots or where they receive morning sun and afternoon shade. A wall or house can provide the dappled light they love. If your garden has clay soil, that's actually a bonus—it retains moisture better than sandy soil.

### 2. Improve Soil Moisture

Add organic matter like compost or leaf mould to improve water retention. Mulching around the base helps keep roots cool and damp. During dry spells, water deeply and regularly, especially in the first two years.

### 3. Select Resilient Varieties

Some hydrangeas are more drought-tolerant than others. Look for varieties like Hydrangea paniculata 'Limelight' or 'Pinky Winky', which handle sun better than mophead types. Hydrangea arborescens 'Annabelle' is also a tough choice.

### 4. Prune and Feed Wisely

Avoid heavy pruning in summer—it stresses plants. Feed with a balanced fertilizer in spring, but not too much nitrogen, which encourages leafy growth at the expense of blooms.

## How to Save Heat-Stressed Hydrangeas

If your hydrangeas are already looking brown and crispy, don't despair. Here's what to do:

- **Water deeply** in the morning or evening, avoiding wetting the leaves.
- **Provide temporary shade** with a parasol or shade cloth during peak heat.
- **Remove damaged blooms** to encourage new growth.
- **Don't fertilize** during a heatwave—it can stress plants further.
