## 1. Deadhead Faded Blooms, But Don't Cut Back Foliage

Deadheading is the most important task for agapanthus in August. Once the flower heads have completely faded, use clean, sharp secateurs to remove the entire flowering stem. Follow the stem down to the base and cut just above where it emerges from the clump, taking care not to damage the crown or surrounding foliage.

Why not cut back the leaves? Those healthy green leaves are crucial for absorbing sunlight and feeding the roots, which helps direct energy toward continued flowering. So, leave the foliage intact—it's doing important work behind the scenes.

## 2. Water Consistently, But Don't Overwater

Agapanthus are drought-tolerant once established, but they still need consistent moisture during their blooming period. Water deeply but infrequently, allowing the soil to dry slightly between waterings. Overwatering can lead to root rot, so always check the soil moisture before adding more water.

If you're dealing with hosepipe bans or hot weather, a DIY irrigation kit can help deliver water directly to the roots, reducing waste. Just ensure the soil drains well—agapanthus hate soggy feet.

**Wedding tip:** If you're using agapanthus in your wedding decor, remember that fresh cuts need water. For centerpieces, consider a fake flowers centerpiece that mimics agapanthus—it's a low-maintenance alternative that still looks stunning.

## 3. Ensure Plenty of Sunshine

Agapanthus thrive in full sun, so make sure they get at least 6-8 hours of direct sunlight daily. In August, the sun is strong, but that's exactly what these plants love. If you're growing them in pots, rotate them occasionally to ensure even growth.

Sunshine not only keeps the plant healthy but also encourages more blooms. If your agapanthus is in a partially shaded spot, consider moving it to a sunnier location—though do this in autumn or spring, not during the heat of summer.

**Decor inspiration:** The vibrant blue of agapanthus can be a color theme for your wedding. Pair fresh or silk flowers wedding decor in similar shades for a cohesive look.

## 4. Hold Off Feeding Agapanthus in Summer

It might be tempting to fertilize your agapanthus to encourage more blooms, but resist the urge. Feeding in August can promote leafy growth at the expense of flowers, and it can also stress the plant during hot weather. Instead, wait until early spring to apply a balanced fertilizer.

If your agapanthus are in containers, they may need a little extra care, but still avoid feeding in summer. Your plant will thank you with a longer blooming period.

## 5. Don't Divide Agapanthus in Summer

Dividing agapanthus is best done in spring or autumn, not in summer. Dividing during the heat can shock the plant and reduce blooming. If you're thinking about propagating, wait until the weather cools down.
