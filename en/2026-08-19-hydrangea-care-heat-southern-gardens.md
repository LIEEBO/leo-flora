## Why Hydrangeas Struggle in Heat

Hydrangeas are naturally woodland plants, thriving in light shade and moist, well-drained soil. That's why heatwaves and droughts can be tough on them. Guy Barter, RHS chief horticultural advisor, explains that RHS Wisley's dry, sandy soil puts some varieties under pressure. Rather than constantly watering, the RHS is considering relocating to northern gardens where the climate is cooler and the soil retains moisture better.

But this doesn't mean southern gardeners (or brides) need to despair. Many hydrangeas are already growing in favorable spots – north-facing slopes, clay soil, or partial shade from walls and houses. These microclimates can keep your plants happy even when temperatures soar.

## Choosing the Right Varieties for Southern Gardens

Not all hydrangeas are equal when it comes to heat tolerance. Some species are better suited to drier conditions. For example, Hydrangea paniculata and Hydrangea arborescens are more adaptable than the bigleaf hydrangeas (Hydrangea macrophylla). When planting, choose varieties that are known for resilience, and place them where they'll get morning sun and afternoon shade.

If you're creating **wedding venue decoration ideas** with hydrangeas, consider using these hardy types in your garden or as cut flowers. They'll last longer and look beautiful in bouquets, centerpieces, and arch decorations.

## Practical Tips for Keeping Hydrangeas Alive in Heat

Here are some expert-approved strategies to help your hydrangeas thrive:

- **Water deeply and consistently** – Hydrangeas need about 1 inch of water per week, but during heatwaves, increase to 2 inches. Water at the base, not overhead, to prevent leaf scorch.
- **Mulch generously** – Apply a 2-3 inch layer of organic mulch around the base to retain soil moisture and keep roots cool.
- **Provide afternoon shade** – Use shade cloth, a patio umbrella, or plant near a fence or wall that blocks intense afternoon sun.
- **Prune wisely** – Avoid heavy pruning in summer; only remove dead or damaged stems.
- **Consider container gardening** – If your soil is too dry, grow hydrangeas in pots with moisture-retentive compost, and move them to shaded areas during peak heat.
