# Garden Ceremony to Intimate Evening: Wedding Venue Decoration Ideas

Imagine exchanging vows surrounded by a vibrant garden, then transitioning to a cozy, candlelit reception under the stars. This is exactly what happened at the stunning Bastide du Roy, and it’s a trend we’re seeing more and more: weddings that tell a story through two distinct atmospheres. If you’re planning your own celebration, this real wedding offers a treasure trove of inspiration. From bold ceremony colors to a soft, romantic reception palette, here’s how to recreate this magic—and how artificial flowers can make it all easier, more beautiful, and budget-friendly.

## The Art of the Two-Act Color Palette

The key to this wedding’s success was its deliberate color story. The ceremony embraced a joyful explosion of pink, coral, blue, and fresh green—colors that felt alive and expressive, perfectly complementing the natural garden setting. But as the sun set, the reception transformed into a more restrained, intimate scene with ivory, cream, and soft neutrals. This shift not only marked the transition from day to night but also created a sense of evolution and intimacy.

**For your own wedding:** Don’t be afraid to use two palettes. Let your ceremony colors be bold and energetic, and then let the reception feel calm and elegant. This contrast will make both spaces feel intentional and memorable.

## How to Create a Stunning Wedding Flower Wall Backdrop

One of the most impactful elements in any wedding is a flower wall. At Bastide du Roy, the lush greenery and blooms created a natural focal point. But you don’t need a real garden to achieve this look. A **wedding flower wall backdrop** can be the star of your ceremony or photo booth—and it’s easier to create than you think.

**DIY tip:** Use a lightweight wooden or PVC frame, attach a grid of chicken wire or zip ties, and then insert stems of **wholesale silk flowers**. Mix large blooms like peonies and roses with smaller fillers and trailing greenery. The best part? You can build it weeks in advance, and it will look perfect on the day—no wilting, no last-minute scrambling.

## Wedding Venue Decoration Ideas for Indoor-Outdoor Flow

If your venue has both outdoor and indoor spaces, take a cue from this wedding. The ceremony celebrated the outdoors, while the reception moved inside (or under a tent) for a more intimate feel. Here are some **wedding venue decoration ideas** to bridge the two:

- **Create a floral arch at the entrance** to the reception area, echoing the ceremony’s style but in the softer reception palette.
- **Use tall, dramatic centerpieces** with cascading silk flowers to draw the eye upward and add elegance to an indoor space.
- **Drape sheer fabric and string lights** to soften the transition from garden to interior.
- **Carry the color story through** with table linens, napkins, and candle holders that match your evening palette.

## Practical Tips for a Flawless Transition

To pull off a two-act wedding like this one, here are a few practical tips:

1. **Plan your timeline:** Schedule the ceremony to end at golden hour, so the transition to evening feels natural and magical.
2. **Have a clear plan for moving decor:** If you’re using the same venue, assign a trusted friend or coordinator to move key pieces (like the flower wall) from ceremony to reception.
3. **Invest in a few statement pieces:** Instead of many small arrangements, focus on one or two large installations—like a flower wall or an arbor—that will have maximum impact.
4. **Lighting is everything:** Use candles, fairy lights, and lanterns to create that intimate glow. Silk flowers look even more beautiful by candlelight.
