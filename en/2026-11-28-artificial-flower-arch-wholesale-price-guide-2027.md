## Why Two Quotes for the Same Arch Differ by 3x

Most price gaps come from the **quote unit**, not quality. Chinese suppliers quote per meter of garland, per horn pair, per set, or per “piece” that is actually one side of the arch. A 2.2m round arch needs 5.5–6.5 meters of garland. At $29 per meter, that arch is $160–189. A buyer who reads “$29” and assumes “$29 per arch” will think every complete arch is overpriced.

Other factors that drive price discrepancies:

- **Flower quality:** Real-touch roses (PU or latex) cost more than basic silk but offer superior realism and durability. Artificial Austin roses with many petals require more material and labor.
- **Frame construction:** Powder-coated steel vs. lightweight aluminum; collapsible vs. fixed. Rental fleets need frames that survive repeated assembly.
- **Density:** Clusters per meter directly affect material cost and labor time. A 5-layer rose arch uses nearly double the flowers of a 3-layer one.
- **Finishing:** Wrapped stems, hidden mechanics, and color-matched ties add labor.

When comparing quotes, always ask for the **total per-arch price** including frame, flowers, and finishing. Request a sample or detailed spec sheet to verify density and materials.

## Where Your $100 Actually Goes

Breaking down a typical $100 arch (e.g., 2.0m round with 4-layer roses):

- **Flowers & foliage: 40–50%** – Silk or real-touch roses, fillers, and greenery. Real-touch roses wholesale can add 15–20% to material cost but extend lifespan.
- **Frame & mechanics: 15–20%** – Steel or aluminum frame, connectors, and base. Collapsible designs cost more but save on freight.
- **Labor: 20–25%** – Hand-assembly, gluing, and shaping. Dense arches with artificial Austin roses take significantly more time.
- **Packaging: 5–10%** – Custom boxes, corner protectors, and moisture barriers for sea freight.
- **Factory overhead & margin: 10–15%** – Includes QC, storage, and profit.

Understanding this split helps you negotiate: if you need a lower price, discuss reducing density or simplifying the frame—not squeezing the factory margin.

## The One Spec That Decides Whether You Got What You Paid For

**Flower density** (clusters per meter) is the single most important specification. It determines visual fullness and perceived value. Always request the clusters-per-meter count and compare against the table above. A 2.2m arch with only 25 clusters/m will look sparse next to a 35 clusters/m version, even if both are described as “full.”

Other critical specs:

- **Material type:** Real-touch PU roses offer the best realism and durability for high-end events; silk is cost-effective for single-use or photo shoots; UV-resistant PE is essential for outdoor arches.
- **Frame gauge:** For rental arches, insist on 1.2mm+ steel thickness.
- **Color fastness:** Ask for light-fastness ratings if the arch will be exposed to sunlight.
- **Assembly method:** Collapsible frames reduce shipping volume and are easier to store.

## Landed Cost: Freight Changes the Answer More Than the Unit Price

A $100 arch can become $135–$240 landed, depending on freight mode and volume. Sea freight adds $15–35 per arch; express adds $70–140. For bulk orders, sea freight is almost always the better choice. But freight is calculated on **CBM (cubic meters)**, not weight, so packaging efficiency matters.

Tips to reduce landed cost:

- **Choose collapsible frames** to minimize CBM.
- **Order in multiples** that fill a pallet or container efficiently.
- **Consolidate with other floral items** (e.g., artificial roses wholesale, real touch flowers wholesale) to maximize container space.
- **Request a CBM quote** with your per-arch price to see the true landed cost.
