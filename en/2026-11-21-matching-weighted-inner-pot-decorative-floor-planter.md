# How to Match a Weighted Inner Pot with a Decorative Floor Planter

When you unbox a tall artificial tree, you might notice the plain black pot at the base. The tree looks realistic, but the pot doesn't. That's because the pot it comes in and the planter you want to display are designed for two different purposes. One is a support base; the other is furniture. Understanding this distinction makes choosing an outer planter for your artificial tree straightforward—it's all about proportion, rim width, and floor contact.

## Why a Weighted Pot Is Not the Final Display Planter

A faux plant arrives in a weighted pot for a simple reason: the tree needs to stand upright before you decide how it should look. For a 185cm artificial bird of paradise, the weighted pot serves as a support base. It sits low, carries the mass, and keeps the trunk upright so the real-touch foliage can spread to about 95cm without leaning. That's a structural job, which is why the base is heavy rather than pretty.

The decorative planter does the opposite job. It defines the rim you see, the finish you touch, and how the tree meets the floor. You look at the outer planter every day; the weighted pot underneath just needs to disappear inside it. Once you grasp this split, matching becomes simple. You're not buying a planter to hold soil or roots—there are none. You're buying a visible shape, a rim width, and a surface that suits the room.

This is also why many artificial indoor potted plants ship with a plain base. Manufacturers build a base that survives transport and holds weight, then leave the visible finish open because finish is a taste decision. Published details for this bird of paradise cover the 185cm height, the 95cm spread, and the real-touch foliage, while the inner pot is described only as weighted. So plan the outer planter around the base you can actually see and measure, not around a number you found online.

## How Planter Width and Tree Height Affect Visual Balance

Visual balance around a tall tree comes down to one comparison: the width of the pot rim against the width of the foliage above it. A 185cm tree with a 95cm spread carries a lot of visual weight up high. If the outer planter is noticeably narrower than that spread, the tree reads as pinched and top-heavy, like a flower stem in a bud vase. If the rim is dramatically wider than the leaves, the tree starts to look short and shrub-like, and the pot becomes the feature instead of the plant.

A reliable middle ground is a floor planter whose rim is clearly wider than the inner pot but not wider than the foliage spread. As a rule of thumb, aim for a rim diameter that is about one-third to one-half of the foliage spread. For a 95cm spread, that means a planter with a rim between 30cm and 45cm. This range keeps the tree looking stable and proportional.

Also consider the height of the planter. A taller planter will make the tree look shorter, while a shorter planter emphasizes the tree's height. For a 185cm tree, a planter height of 30-40cm works well, lifting the foliage to eye level without overwhelming the space.

## Practical Tips for Choosing the Right Planter

- **Measure the inner pot:** Before shopping, measure the diameter and height of the weighted pot. This ensures the outer planter will fit over it comfortably.
- **Check the material:** For a natural look, choose planters made of ceramic, terracotta, or woven materials. For a modern vibe, metal or concrete works well.
- **Consider the room's style:** Match the planter finish to your existing decor. A rustic room might suit a distressed finish, while a minimalist space calls for clean lines.
- **Ensure stability:** A heavier planter provides a lower center of gravity, reducing the risk of tipping. If the planter is lightweight, add weight inside (like sand or stones) around the inner pot.
- **Don't forget drainage:** Since there's no soil, drainage isn't necessary, but a planter with a sealed bottom will protect your floors from scratches.
