## Why Payment Terms Matter for Your Wedding Flowers

When you’re sourcing artificial flowers, especially from overseas suppliers, the payment structure is just as important as choosing the right peonies. Most first-time buyers ask, “How much deposit do you need, and what if the goods never arrive?” That’s a smart question. A well-structured payment term protects you, while a poorly written one leaves you vulnerable.

The standard industry practice is a 30/70 T/T split: 30% deposit to start production, and 70% balance after inspection passes. This gives the factory enough capital to buy materials, while you keep leverage until you’ve verified the quality. Never pay more than 30% upfront on a first order—if a supplier demands 50% or more, it’s a red flag that they may lack working capital.

For DIY brides, this means you can secure your order without handing over your entire budget. And for planners, it ensures you have recourse if something goes wrong. ## Affordable Wedding Flowers: Where to Save and Where to Splurge

Artificial flowers are inherently more budget-friendly than fresh ones, but you can stretch your dollar further with smart choices. Here’s how to allocate your floral budget:

- **Save on greenery and fillers:** Fake eucalyptus, ivy, and ferns are incredibly realistic and cost a fraction of fresh. Use them generously to add volume.
- **Splurge on focal flowers:** Invest in high-quality silk roses or peonies for your **fake flowers centerpiece**—they’ll be the star of the table.
- **Mix and match:** Combine artificial flowers with fresh elements like candles or greenery for a look that feels lush without the price tag.

When sourcing, look for suppliers who offer sample packs so you can check quality before committing. Many reputable factories, like yiwuleoflowers, provide detailed proforma invoices that break down costs, including shipping and HS codes, so there are no surprises.

## 5 Red Flags to Watch for in a Payment Request

Before you send any money, scan the payment request for these warning signs:

1. **Deposit over 30%** – As mentioned, this shifts too much risk to you.
2. **No inspection clause** – Always insist on a quality check before paying the balance.
3. **Payment to a personal account** – Legitimate factories use company accounts.
4. **Vague product specs** – If the invoice doesn’t detail materials, sizes, and colors, walk away.
5. **Pressure to pay quickly** – Reputable suppliers give you time to review terms.

## How to Structure Payment on Your First Order

If you’re ordering artificial flowers for the first time, follow this simple framework:

1. **Request a proforma invoice** that clearly states the 30/70 split, inspection terms, and delivery timeline.
2. **Pay the 30% deposit** only after confirming product details and lead time.
3. **Arrange a third-party inspection** or request photos/videos before releasing the 70% balance.
4. **Pay against the bill of lading copy** to ensure goods are shipped.

This approach gives you peace of mind and ensures your wedding flowers arrive as expected. For planners ordering in bulk, consider negotiating a slightly lower deposit if you have a long-term relationship—but never skip the inspection step.
