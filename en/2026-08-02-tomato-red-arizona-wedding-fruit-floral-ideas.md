## How to Design with Fresh Produce and Silk Flowers

If you're inspired by this tomato-red theme, here's how to make it work for your own wedding. Start by choosing a color palette that complements your venue and season. For a summer or fall wedding, think deep reds, oranges, and greens. Then, mix in fresh elements like cherry tomatoes, figs, artichokes, or even small chili peppers. To keep costs down, consider using affordable wedding flowers like baby's breath or carnations as fillers, and invest in a few statement blooms like garden roses or dahlias.

But here's a pro tip: not all produce is built to last through a long reception. That's where wholesale silk flowers come in. You can create stunning, realistic-looking fruit and veggie accents using silk versions of items like berries, oranges, or even leafy greens. These can be used for ceremony arches, backdrop installations, or any area that needs to stay fresh-looking for hours. By mixing fresh and faux, you get the best of both worlds—the vibrant look of real produce and the durability of silk.

## Practical Tips for a Fruit-and-Veggie Floral Theme

Ready to try this trend? Here are some actionable tips to help you pull it off:

- **Choose sturdy produce:** Opt for items that can withstand heat and handling, like citrus fruits, apples, pomegranates, or artichokes. Avoid soft berries that might bruise easily.
- **Use floral picks and skewers:** Secure fruits and veggies to floral picks or bamboo skewers before inserting them into your arrangements. This keeps them in place and prevents sliding.
- **Cut stems at an angle:** For produce with stems (like grapes or cherry tomatoes), cut them at an angle and place them in water tubes to keep them hydrated.
- **Mix in silk flowers for longevity:** For pieces that need to last all day, like bouquets or centerpieces, consider using artificial flowers for wedding decor. Modern silk flowers look incredibly realistic and can be reused or resold after the event.
- **Think about the scent:** Some fruits have a strong fragrance that might not blend well with your floral scents. Test a few combinations beforehand to ensure a pleasant aroma.
