If you're a DIY bride or wedding planner, you know hydrangeas are a wedding floral favorite. Their lush, romantic clusters add instant charm to bouquets, centerpieces, and ceremony arches. But if you're growing your own hydrangeas for your big day, you need to know how to care for them after they bloom—otherwise, you might sacrifice next year's flowers. Let's dive into the best practices for hydrangea pruning so your garden (and your wedding decor) stays blooming beautiful.

## Why Pruning Timing Matters for Hydrangeas

Hydrangeas fall into two main categories: those that bloom on old wood and those that bloom on new wood. Old-wood bloomers, like bigleaf (Hydrangea macrophylla), oakleaf (Hydrangea quercifolia), and mountain hydrangeas (Hydrangea serrata), form their flower buds during the previous summer and fall. If you prune these too late in the season, you'll cut off next year's buds—and that means fewer flowers for your wedding.

New-wood bloomers, such as panicle and smooth hydrangeas, flower on the current season's growth, so they're more forgiving. But for old-wood types, pruning timing is critical. Most experts recommend pruning right after they finish flowering, typically before August. If you miss that window, it's safer to skip pruning altogether than to risk losing next year's blooms.

## How to Identify Old-Wood Hydrangeas

Before you pick up your pruners, identify what type of hydrangea you have. Old-wood varieties include:

- **Bigleaf hydrangeas** – Known for mophead (large, round clusters) and lacecap (flat, delicate blooms) forms.
- **Oakleaf hydrangeas** – Easy to spot with their oak-shaped leaves and cone-shaped flower clusters.
- **Mountain hydrangeas** – Similar to bigleaf but often with smaller leaves and lacecap-style flowers.

If you're unsure, a conservative approach is best. Skip pruning this year, and you'll still have plenty of blooms. If you need to shape the plant, remove only dead or damaged stems, and do it immediately after flowering.

## Pruning Tips for a Bountiful Wedding Season

For DIY brides growing hydrangeas for their wedding, here are some practical tips:

- **Prune right after flowering** – Once the blooms fade, cut back the stems to a healthy pair of buds. This encourages new growth and protects next year's buds.
- **Avoid late-summer pruning** – By August, buds for next year are already forming. Pruning then will remove them.
- **Remove dead wood only** – If you're unsure, just remove dead or weak stems. This keeps the plant healthy without compromising blooms.
- **Consider reblooming varieties** – Some newer bigleaf and mountain hydrangeas bloom on both old and new wood, making them more forgiving. Look for labels like 'Endless Summer' or 'Let's Dance'.
