# What Does 'Quantity 1' Mean When Buying Artificial Flowers?

If you've ever ordered artificial flowers online, you've probably stared at a product page wondering: *Does 'quantity 1' mean one stem, one bunch, or one full bouquet?* It's one of the most common — and most costly — sources of confusion for DIY brides and wedding planners alike.

A single peony stem and a six-stem maple set can both be listed as "quantity 1," yet they deliver very different amounts of product. Understanding how stem counts work before you click "buy" is the key to ordering the right amount of **realistic artificial flowers** for your big day — without overspending or coming up short.

## Why Stem Counts Matter for Wedding Florals

When you're building **faux floral arrangements** for a wedding, every stem counts. A bridal bouquet might need 15–25 stems depending on the flower type and desired fullness. Centerpieces could need 8–12 stems each. Multiply that across ten tables, and a misunderstanding about "quantity 1" can throw your entire budget and timeline off course.

The good news? Once you know what to look for, ordering artificial flowers becomes predictable and stress-free.

## How to Read 'Quantity 1' Correctly

Here's what to check on every product listing before you order:

### 1. Look at the Unit Description

Product pages usually specify whether "quantity 1" means one stem, one bunch, or one set. Look for phrases like "1 stem," "1 bunch (6 stems)," or "set of 6." If it's unclear, that's your cue to ask the seller directly.

### 2. Count the Stems, Not the Photos

A listing photo might show a lush arrangement of ten peonies — but "quantity 1" could mean just one stem. Always read the fine print rather than judging by the styled image.

### 3. Check the Height and Bloom Size

Stem length and flower head diameter tell you how much visual impact each piece delivers. A 20-inch stem with a 3-inch bloom fills space differently than a petite 12-inch spray.

### 4. Confirm the Color and Variant

Double-check the selected color and variant before checkout. Many returns happen simply because the wrong shade was ordered — especially with **bridal bouquet artificial flowers**, where ivory, blush, and cream can look very different in person.

## Practical Tips for DIY Brides and Planners

- **Order a sample first.** Buy one stem of each flower type you're considering. It's the best way to judge color, texture, and realism before committing to bulk.
- **Buy 10–15% extra.** Stems get bent, blooms get misplaced, and last-minute additions happen. A small buffer saves you from scrambling.
- **Standardize your supplier.** Mixing stem counts from different vendors invites confusion. Working with one reliable source — such as artificial flowers from leofaux — keeps sizing and color consistent across your entire order.
- **Keep a stem-count spreadsheet.** List each arrangement, the number of stems needed, and the unit type. This simple habit prevents costly miscalculations.
- **Ask about bunch sizes for bulk orders.** If you're ordering for a large wedding, ask whether stems are sold individually or in pre-made bunches, as bulk pricing often differs.
