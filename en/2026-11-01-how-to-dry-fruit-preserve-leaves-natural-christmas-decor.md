## Bring Nature Indoors: A Guide to Drying Fruit and Preserving Leaves for Christmas

There’s something magical about filling your home with natural elements during the holidays. Dried citrus, crimson cranberries, and glossy magnolia leaves add warmth, texture, and a cozy, classic feel that store-bought decorations just can’t match. Whether you’re a DIY bride planning a winter wedding or a wedding planner looking for sustainable décor ideas, learning to dry fruit and preserve leaves is a skill worth mastering. Plus, these natural touches pair beautifully with artificial flowers for wedding arrangements, creating a timeless, organic look.

In this guide, we’ll walk you through the step-by-step process of drying oranges, cranberries, and pomegranates, and preserving magnolia leaves and boxwoods. We’ll also share practical tips for incorporating these elements into your Christmas décor and wedding designs.

## Why Use Dried Fruit and Preserved Leaves?

Dried fruit and preserved leaves offer a rustic, natural aesthetic that complements both traditional and modern themes. They’re cost-effective, eco-friendly, and can be reused year after year if stored properly. For weddings, they add a touch of seasonal charm—think dried orange slices in a wedding flower wall backdrop or preserved magnolia leaves woven into table runners. ## Step-by-Step: How to Dry Oranges, Cranberries, and Pomegranates

### Drying Oranges

1. **Prep the fruit:** Wash and slice oranges into 1/8-inch thick rounds. Pat dry with a paper towel.
2. **Oven method:** Preheat oven to 200°F (93°C). Place slices on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Bake for 2–3 hours, flipping halfway, until dry and leathery.
3. **Air-drying:** For a slower method, thread slices onto a string and hang in a dry, well-ventilated area for 1–2 weeks.
4. **Storage:** Store in an airtight container once completely dry.

### Drying Cranberries

1. **Prep:** Rinse and dry cranberries thoroughly. Cut them in half for faster drying.
2. **Oven method:** Spread on a baking sheet and bake at 200°F (93°C) for 2–3 hours, stirring occasionally.
3. **Air-drying:** Use a dehydrator set to 135°F (57°C) for 6–8 hours, or air-dry in a warm, dry room for several days.
4. **Tip:** Cranberries can be sticky; toss with a little cornstarch before storing.

### Drying Pomegranates

1. **Prep:** Cut pomegranates into thin slices (1/4-inch thick). Remove seeds if desired.
2. **Oven method:** Bake at 200°F (93°C) for 3–4 hours, flipping once.
3. **Air-drying:** Place slices on a rack in a dry, sunny spot for 1–2 weeks.
4. **Note:** Pomegranate slices may darken; this is normal and adds to the rustic look.

## Preserving Magnolia Leaves and Boxwoods

### Preserving Magnolia Leaves

Magnolia leaves are a favorite for their glossy green tops and velvety brown undersides. To preserve them:

1. **Glycerin method:** Mix 1 part glycerin with 2 parts water. Submerge leaves in the solution for 2–3 weeks until they feel pliable.
2. **Pressing:** For a flat look, press leaves between newspaper and heavy books for 2–3 weeks.
3. **Tip:** Preserved magnolia leaves retain their color and flexibility, making them ideal for wreaths and garlands.

### Preserving Boxwoods

Boxwood sprigs add a classic touch to holiday décor. To preserve:

1. **Air-drying:** Bundle sprigs and hang upside down in a dark, dry area for 2–3 weeks.
2. **Glycerin method:** Follow the same ratio as magnolia leaves, submerging sprigs for 1–2 weeks.
3. **Note:** Boxwoods may lose some color; consider painting them with a light coat of green floral spray if desired.

## Practical Tips for Using Dried Fruit and Preserved Leaves in Décor

- **Wreaths and garlands:** Combine dried orange slices, cranberries, and preserved leaves with artificial pine picks for a lasting wreath.
- **Table settings:** Scatter dried fruit and leaves around candles or use them as place card holders.
- **Wedding flower wall backdrop:** Incorporate dried citrus and preserved magnolia leaves into a flower wall for a natural, textured look. Pair with wholesale silk flowers to fill gaps and add volume.
- **Tree ornaments:** Thread dried fruit slices onto twine and hang on the tree.
- **Gift wrapping:** Use preserved leaves as gift toppers for a handmade touch.
