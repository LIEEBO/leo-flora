# Who Sets Up Wedding Decorations? Venue vs Planner vs Florist vs DIY

It's a question couples often ask too late: The flowers have arrived, the tables are empty, the ceremony starts in three hours—and suddenly everyone is asking, "Wait…who is actually setting all of this up?"

Wedding décor may be planned months in advance, but deciding who will unpack, assemble, position, adjust, and eventually remove everything is a separate job. The good news: you have four main options—your venue, your planner, your florist, or you and your crew. Here's how to choose the right setup team, plus practical tips for using **realistic artificial flowers** to make the day easier.

## What Does “Setup” Actually Include?

Setup isn't just placing centerpieces. It usually covers:

- Unpacking and sorting deliveries
- Assembling arches, backdrops, and installations
- Setting tables: linens, chargers, glassware, candles, florals
- Positioning signage, gifts, guest book, and card box
- Lighting check and final fluffing of flowers
- Teardown and packing at the end of the night

Who does this depends on your contracts. Always ask: "Is setup and teardown included, and what exactly is covered?"

## Option 1: The Venue Handles Setup

Many venues include basic setup: tables, chairs, linens, and standard layout. Some also allow you to drop off décor in advance and their team will place it.

**Pros:** Convenient, often included, staff knows the space.
**Cons:** Limited to simple items; they may not assemble complex installations or handle personal flowers.

**Tip:** Ask for a written setup list. If you're using **realistic artificial flowers**, venues often prefer them because they're durable, don't shed, and can be pre-arranged—saving setup time.

## Option 2: Your Planner or Coordinator Takes Over

A full-service planner typically manages vendor load-in and décor setup. A day-of coordinator may only direct vendors, not physically set up.

**Pros:** One point of contact, timeline management, problem-solving.
**Cons:** Higher cost; setup scope varies by contract.

**Tip:** Clarify whether your planner will physically place centerpieces or just supervise. If you're DIY-ing florals, a coordinator can place pre-made arrangements quickly.

## Option 3: Your Florist Sets Up Flowers

Florists often deliver and install ceremony arches, aisle flowers, and reception centerpieces. Some include teardown; many don't.

**Pros:** Professional styling, proper handling of delicate blooms.
**Cons:** Delivery/setup fees, limited to floral items, teardown may cost extra.

## Option 4: DIY Setup with Family and Friends

DIY can save money and give you full control—but it requires a realistic plan.

**Pros:** Budget-friendly, personal, flexible.
**Cons:** Labor-intensive, time-sensitive, relies on helpers.

**DIY Setup Tips:**

1. **Create a diagram** for each table and area. Label boxes by table number.
2. **Pre-assemble everything** at home: centerpieces, bouquets, signage, card box.
3. **Use artificial flowers** to eliminate water, wilting, and refrigeration needs.
4. **Assign roles:** one person for ceremony, one for reception, one for signage.
5. **Pack a kit:** scissors, zip ties, tape, glue dots, touch-up paint, lint roller.
6. **Schedule a 2-hour buffer** before guests arrive for adjustments.
7. **Plan teardown** the same night—or hire a cleanup crew.

## Who Should Set Up Your Wedding? A Quick Decision Guide

- **Venue setup included?** Start there; supplement with a florist for flowers.
- **Complex installations?** Hire a planner or florist.
- **Tight budget?** DIY with pre-made artificial arrangements.
- **Destination wedding?** Choose vendors who handle setup and teardown.
