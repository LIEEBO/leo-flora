# Hosting a Wedding or Big Celebration at Home in Nashville: How to Prepare Your Space Before the Decor Goes In

Nashville knows how to celebrate at home. Ceremonies under a backyard oak, receptions spilling from the kitchen onto a wide front porch, a first dance in the living room with a friend on guitar—because this is Music City, after all.

But walk into enough homes the week before a big party, and the same problems show up again and again. In Middle Tennessee, the heat, humidity, and spring storm season make every one of them a little worse. Picture an East Nashville home getting ready for a June wedding. Florals ordered, rentals booked, playlist ready. Then the trouble starts—and it almost always comes from one of four places: the walls, the floors, the power, and the entry.

Three of these have quick fixes. One does not. Here is how to handle each before the decor goes in.

## The Walls: Start Earlier Than You Think

Walls are the backdrop to every photo, so they matter far more at a celebration than on an ordinary Tuesday. The good news? Most wall problems are cosmetic. A melamine sponge lifts scuffs. Small dings need a dab of spackle and touch-up paint. Faded patches behind furniture usually need one fresh coat, not a full repaint.

Nashville adds a twist: humid air slows paint drying, so a coat that would feel finished overnight in a dry climate can stay tacky and smell longer through a Tennessee summer. The EPA lists paint among common sources of volatile organic compounds, noting indoor levels can run up to ten times higher than outdoors. So paint early, choose a low-VOC formula, and run fans with windows open whenever possible. Nobody wants the reception to smell like a hardware store.

Once walls are clean and dry, they become a canvas. This is where a **wedding flower wall backdrop** shines—especially in artificial blooms. A lush panel of faux roses, peonies, and greenery transforms a plain wall into a photo moment without risking water damage or wilting in the Nashville heat. ## The Floors: The One Without a Quick Fix

You cannot touch up a lifting plank or carpet worn through to the backing. And floors take more punishment than anything else during an event. Rental chairs drag. Tables shift. A crowd line dances in heels and boots. Nashville’s long, humid summers make natural wood swell and shrink, which is why older floors start to gap, cup, or lift at the edges. Add red clay tracked in from the yard, and tired floors give out fast.

Worn floors also create real hazards. Raised edges and loose transitions catch toes, and safety council data shows falls are the leading cause of preventable injury deaths among adults 65 and older. Grandparents will cross those floors in their best shoes, often after dark. If your floors are beyond a quick fix, schedule new flooring installation well before the wedding—at least a month out, so the new surface settles and any finish odors fade.

In the meantime, protect what you have. Felt pads on every chair and table leg. Runners in high-traffic paths. A no-shoes rule for setup crews. And when you place that **fake flowers centerpiece** on each table, use a felt base or a small mat underneath to prevent scratches from vases and stands.

## The Power: Plan for the Load

Air conditioning, a live band, string lights, and caterers’ warming trays all fighting for the same outlets is a recipe for a mid-reception blackout. Before you plug anything in, map your outlets. Know which circuits handle which rooms. If you are renting a band or a photo booth, ask what they need. Consider a portable generator for outdoor lighting and a few heavy-duty extension cords with safety covers for walkways.

For decor, LED string lights and battery-operated candles draw almost nothing, so they are ideal for a home wedding. They also pair beautifully with artificial arrangements—no heat, no water, no wilting. If you are building a **wedding flower wall backdrop** or a **fake flowers centerpiece** collection, battery-powered fairy lights woven through the blooms add warmth without overloading a single circuit.

## The Entry: Keep the Mud Outside

One stormy afternoon, and the front door becomes a mud path into the reception. Nashville’s spring storms are no joke. Prepare a landing zone: a sturdy outdoor mat, a boot tray, and a basket of towels. If you expect rain, lay a runner from the door to the main room and have a designated spot for wet umbrellas. A small tent or awning over the entry steps can save the day.

This is also where artificial flowers earn their keep. Real petals and greenery turn to slime when wet and tracked indoors. Faux blooms stay pristine, so you can decorate the entry with a welcoming arrangement that survives whatever the weather does.
