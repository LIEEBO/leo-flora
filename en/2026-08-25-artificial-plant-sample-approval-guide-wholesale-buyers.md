When you’re sourcing artificial flowers for wedding décor, the sample approval stage is where your vision transforms into a tangible, production-ready reality. For wholesale buyers—whether you’re a wedding planner stocking up on wholesale silk flowers or a DIY bride curating your own bridal bouquet artificial flowers—this step is crucial. A sample isn’t just a pretty preview; it’s your quality benchmark, your color reference, and your contract with the supplier. In this guide, we’ll walk you through how to master the sample approval process, ensuring your floral orders bloom exactly as you envision.

## Why Sample Approval Matters for Wholesale Buyers

Sample approval is your safeguard against costly mistakes. When you order artificial flowers in bulk, you’re committing to hundreds or thousands of stems. A sample lets you verify:

- **Color accuracy**: Artificial flowers can vary in shade due to dye lots and materials.
- **Material quality**: Check if the petals feel realistic or look plasticky.
- **Size and proportion**: Ensure stems match your design specs.
- **Durability**: For wedding décor, flowers need to withstand handling and travel.

Without a proper approval process, you risk receiving a shipment that doesn’t match your expectations—leading to wasted time, money, and stress.

## Step-by-Step Sample Approval Checklist

### 1. Define Your Specifications First

Before requesting samples, write down your exact requirements. Include:

- Flower type and variety (e.g., peonies, roses, hydrangeas)
- Stem length and head diameter
- Color codes (use Pantone or supplier’s color chart)
- Material preferences (silk, polyester, foam, etc.)
- Quantity needed for your project

This clarity helps your supplier produce or select the right sample for you.

### 2. Request Multiple Samples for Comparison

Don’t settle for just one sample. Ask for 2–3 variations of the same flower—different shades, materials, or sizes. This allows you to compare and choose the best match. For bridal bouquet artificial flowers, you might want to see how different whites or blush tones look under various lighting.

### 3. Evaluate the Sample Thoroughly

When your samples arrive, don’t just glance at them. Perform a hands-on inspection:

- **Touch**: Feel the texture. High-quality silk flowers feel soft and natural.
- **Bend**: Gently bend the stem and petals to check flexibility and resilience.
- **Smell**: Some cheap artificial flowers have a chemical odor. Good ones should be odorless.
- **Visual**: Place the sample in different lighting—inside, outside, and under warm/cool bulbs—to see how it photographs.

### 4. Test for Durability

Wedding flowers need to survive a full day of use. Give your sample a mini stress test: squeeze the petals, twist the stem, and even pack it in a box for a day. If it arrives intact and looks good, it’s likely production-ready.

### 5. Document and Communicate Your Approval

Once you’re satisfied, document your approval in writing. Send your supplier a clear email confirming:

- The sample you approve (include photos)
- Any minor adjustments (e.g., “slightly deeper pink”)
- The deadline for production

This creates a reference standard that the factory will follow for bulk orders.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Skipping the sample stage**: Even if you’re on a tight deadline, never skip samples. A rushed order often leads to disappointment.
- **Relying solely on photos**: Photos can be misleading. Always request physical samples.
- **Ignoring color fastness**: Ask if the colors fade over time or with exposure to sunlight.
- **Not checking the return policy**: Understand what happens if the bulk order doesn’t match the approved sample.

## How This Applies to Wedding Floral Projects
