## The 'Handmade' Lie: Why Your Faux Plant's Irregularity Is Machine-Made and the Three Marks That Prove It

As a DIY bride or wedding planner, you've likely scoured product descriptions for that golden word: *handmade*. It suggests artistry, uniqueness, and quality. But in the artificial flower industry, 'handmade' is often just a price tag—not a promise. Many faux plants marketed as handmade are actually mass-produced by machines, with intentional irregularities to mimic a human touch.

At **leofaux**, we believe in transparency. So let's expose the three telltale marks that prove your faux plant's 'irregularity' is machine-made, and how to spot genuine craftsmanship.

### Mark 1: Perfectly Imperfect Repeats

Machine-made faux plants often have a pattern of irregularities that repeats at regular intervals. Look closely at the leaves or petals: if you see the same 'random' notch or vein pattern every few inches, that's a stamping or molding artifact. Handcrafted pieces, by contrast, have truly unique variations because each is shaped individually.

**Tip:** When shopping for artificial flowers for wedding decor, inspect a few stems from the same bunch. If the irregularities are identical, it's machine-made.

### Mark 2: Uniform Edges and Veins

Hand-finishing involves cutting, shaping, and texturing each leaf. Machines, however, use dies that create uniform edges and veins. Run your finger along the edge of a leaf: if it feels consistently smooth or has a repetitive scalloped pattern, it's likely machine-cut. Hand-cut leaves have subtle inconsistencies in edge thickness and vein depth.

**Tip:** For faux floral arrangements that look real, mix machine-made and hand-finished elements. Use machine-made greenery as filler and hand-finished blooms as focal points.

### Mark 3: The Smell of Mass Production

Believe it or not, your nose can detect machine-made faux plants. They often emit a faint chemical or plastic smell from the manufacturing process. Handcrafted silk flowers made with natural materials and dyes have a subtler, sometimes earthy scent. If it smells like a factory, it probably is.

**Tip:** Air out new faux plants for 24-48 hours before your event. If the smell lingers, consider returning them.
