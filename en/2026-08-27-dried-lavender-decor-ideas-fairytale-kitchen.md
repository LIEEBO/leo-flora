## Why Dried Lavender Works in the Kitchen

Dried lavender isn’t just for bathrooms or entryways—it’s a fantastic kitchen accent. Ntola Obazee, creative director at Kutchenhaus, explains: “Dried lavender can be used across the entire home, and we often see it hung in places like the bathroom or on doors. However, what most people don’t automatically think of is using it in the kitchen. If you have a rail above the worktop, or at the end of your cabinet or island, then lavender, along with other dried herbs, is great at adding scent as well as visual interest.”

It creates texture and provides a gentle scent, which helps cover old food smells. Plus, it’s a low-maintenance alternative to fresh flowers, lasting for months. For brides and event planners, this same principle applies to wedding decor—using dried or faux elements can save time and money while still looking stunning.

## How to Get the Look: Hanging Lavender Bunches

Clodagh’s kitchen features a Belfast sink, dark wood finishes, flagstone floors, and open shelving. But the standout is the lavender hanging from ceiling beams, spaced between pots and pans. To recreate this:

- **Use sturdy twine or ribbon** to tie small bunches of dried lavender.
- **Hang them from beams, rails, or hooks**—even a simple curtain rod works.
- **Mix with other dried herbs** like rosemary, thyme, or eucalyptus for variety.
- **Place them near a window** so the scent disperses naturally.

If you’re planning a wedding, consider using faux floral arrangements instead of fresh lavender—they look just as beautiful and can be prepared weeks in advance. Many couples opt for wholesale silk flowers to create lush, budget-friendly decor that lasts beyond the big day.

## Beyond Lavender: Other Dried and Faux Options

Lavender is gorgeous, but don’t stop there. Dried hydrangeas, statice, and even dried citrus slices add rustic charm. For a more permanent solution, faux floral arrangements are perfect for kitchens and weddings alike. They require zero maintenance, never wilt, and can be reused for multiple events.

If you’re a DIY bride, consider mixing dried lavender with affordable wedding flowers like baby’s breath or eucalyptus. You can buy these in bulk from wholesalers or online retailers—look for wholesale silk flowers to get the best value. Use them in centerpieces, bouquets, or hanging installations that echo Clodagh’s style.

## Practical Tips for DIY Brides and Planners

- **Start small**: Test a few bunches in your kitchen or on a wedding arch before committing.
- **Combine textures**: Pair lavender with twine, burlap, or linen for a rustic feel.
- **Use scent strategically**: In a wedding setting, place dried lavender near seating areas to create a calming atmosphere.
- **Invest in quality faux flowers**: Cheap silk can look shiny; choose matte finishes for realism.
- **Plan for longevity**: Faux floral arrangements can be sold or repurposed after the event, making them a smart investment.
