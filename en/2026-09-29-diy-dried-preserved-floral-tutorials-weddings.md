## Introduction

Dried and preserved botanicals are having a major moment in wedding design. They bring texture, depth, and a romantic, everlasting quality that fresh flowers simply can't match. Whether you're a DIY bride planning a rustic celebration or a wedding planner looking for sustainable decor ideas, working with dried and preserved florals opens up a world of creative possibilities.

In this guide, we'll walk through practical DIY tutorials inspired by luxury floral designer Eddie Zaratsian, and show you how to blend dried botanicals with realistic artificial flowers for arrangements that look stunning and last long after the big day.

## Why Dried and Preserved Florals Belong in Your Wedding

Dried flowers — think pampas grass, bleached ferns, poppy pods, and black beard wheat — offer a sculptural, organic look that feels both modern and timeless. Preserved florals keep their softness and color for months or even years, making them ideal for couples who want to repurpose their ceremony decor at the reception or at home.

One of the biggest advantages? Texture. Mixing dried elements with fresh blooms, or with high-quality realistic artificial flowers, creates a layered, designer look that photographs beautifully. And because dried materials don't wilt, you can assemble arrangements days or even weeks ahead of your event — a lifesaver for DIY brides.

## Tutorial 1: Monochromatic Creamy Arrangements

Zaratsian's first tutorial focuses on natural-hued dried and preserved products like poppy pods and bleached ferns, grouped in beautiful pottery vases. The result is a creamy, monochromatic collection that feels elegant and understated.

**How to recreate it:**

- Choose a tight color palette — creams, ivories, and soft beiges work best.
- Combine three to five different dried textures: seed pods, bleached greenery, dried grasses.
- Use pottery or ceramic vases in neutral tones to let the botanicals shine.
- Group arrangements in odd numbers (three or five) for visual balance.

**Pro tip:** If you want to add volume without blowing your budget, supplement dried stems with wholesale silk flowers in matching cream tones. They blend seamlessly and fill out the arrangement.

## Tutorial 2: Swan-Inspired Arrangements with Mixed Textures

Swans represent everlasting love — a perfect motif for weddings. Zaratsian's swan pieces incorporate both dried and fresh flowers, along with non-traditional elements like pampas grass, bleached umbrella ferns, and even feathers.

**How to recreate it:**

- Start with a swan-shaped base or a sculptural vessel.
- Layer dried pampas grass and bleached ferns for movement and softness.
- Tuck in feathers or dried seed pods for unexpected texture.
- Add a few fresh blooms or realistic artificial flowers for a pop of color and life.

This approach works beautifully for a fake flowers centerpiece that guests will admire all night — and you can keep it as a keepsake afterward.

## Tutorial 3: Black Beard Wheat and Dahlia Trio

Perfect for late summer and fall weddings, this dramatic trio mixes fresh dahlias, ranunculus, and greens with dried black beard wheat. The contrast between soft, romantic blooms and structured dried wheat creates a sculptural, high-impact look with just a few components.

**How to recreate it:**

- Use three vessels of varying heights for a dynamic trio.
- Pair one focal flower (dahlias or ranunculus) with dried wheat and greenery.
- Keep the color palette tight — deep burgundy, blush, and wheat gold work beautifully.
- If dahlias are out of season, substitute realistic artificial flowers in the same shade.
