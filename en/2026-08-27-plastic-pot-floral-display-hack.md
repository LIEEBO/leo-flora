## The Viral Hack: Using Plant Pots as Flower Frogs

A recent TikTok video by @jeffandlaurenshow showcases a brilliant method: place a plastic plant pot upside down inside a clear vase, then thread flower stems through the drainage holes. This acts like a traditional flower frog, holding stems securely in place for a fuller, more polished look. It's a simple yet effective way to create a professional-looking arrangement without spending extra money.

Lucy Hook, Plant and Floral Lead at Bloom & Wild, explains, "The drainage holes naturally help to hold your stems in place, giving you a little more structure and support as you arrange. While you won't have quite as much control as you would with a traditional flower frog, it's a lovely way to experiment with arranging and, importantly, means you're giving an old pot a second life rather than sending it to waste."

## How to Choose the Right Pot and Vase

Not all pots are created equal. The shape and pattern of the drainage holes matter. Lucy advises, "A pot with holes arranged in a circular pattern works particularly well in a bowl or wide-necked vase, as it gives you plenty of space to spread your stems out and create a fuller, more natural-looking arrangement."

If the holes are in lines or a less open pattern, your flowers may feel restricted. In that case, you can carefully add extra holes using a drill or a heated screwdriver to give yourself more flexibility. Pairing the right pot with the right vase ensures your arrangement looks balanced and beautiful.

## Practical Tips for Using This Hack

- **Choose the right size**: Make sure the pot fits snugly inside your vase, but leaves enough room for water (if using fresh flowers) or for the stems to fan out.
- **Mix textures and heights**: Combine different flower types and stem lengths for a dynamic display. For a **fake flowers centerpiece**, use a mix of silk blooms and greenery to create depth.
- **Hide the pot**: Use a clear glass vase to keep the pot invisible, or paint the pot to match your decor if you're using an opaque vase.
- **Experiment with placement**: Try using this method for smaller arrangements like bud vases or for larger statement pieces.
