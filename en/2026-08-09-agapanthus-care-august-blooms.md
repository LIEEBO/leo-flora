# Agapanthus Care in August: Simple Jobs for Stunning Blooms

If you're a gardener, you know the joy of seeing agapanthus burst into color during the peak of summer. Those striking blue globes atop tall stems are truly showstoppers. But as August rolls in, you might wonder what to do with agapanthus to keep them looking their best. The good news? You don't need to do much! In fact, agapanthus care in August is all about minimal intervention, letting the plant shine while you enjoy the warm days.

## 1. Deadhead Spent Blooms, But Leave the Foliage Alone

As agapanthus flowers fade, it's tempting to tidy up the whole plant. However, the key is to only remove the spent flower stems. Using clean, sharp secateurs, cut each faded stem right at the base, just above the clump. Be careful not to damage the crown or surrounding leaves.

Why leave the foliage? Those green leaves are solar panels for the plant, absorbing sunlight to feed the roots and encourage more blooms. Removing them would rob the plant of energy. So, resist the urge to cut back leaves in August—that's a job for autumn once flowering has fully finished.

This deadheading not only keeps your garden looking neat but also redirects the plant's energy toward producing new flowers, extending the blooming period. ## 2. Water Consistently, But Don't Overdo It

Agapanthus are drought-tolerant once established, but they still appreciate consistent moisture during active growth and blooming. In August, especially during heatwaves or hosepipe bans, you need to be mindful. Water deeply but less frequently to encourage deep root growth. Aim for about an inch of water per week, either from rainfall or manual watering.

Overwatering can lead to root rot, so ensure the soil drains well. If you're using pots, check that they have drainage holes. A simple trick is to stick your finger into the soil—if it feels dry an inch down, it's time to water. This balanced approach keeps your plants hydrated without drowning them.

## 3. Ensure Plenty of Sunshine

Agapanthus thrive in full sun, which is essential for abundant flowering. In August, the sun is usually plentiful, but if your plants are in a shaded spot, they may not perform as well. Ensure they get at least six hours of direct sunlight daily. If they're in containers, you can move them to sunnier locations as needed.

Sunlight not only fuels photosynthesis but also helps dry the foliage, reducing the risk of fungal diseases. So, position your agapanthus where they can soak up the rays. ## 4. Hold Off on Feeding and Dividing

One of the most common mistakes is to fertilize agapanthus in summer, thinking it will boost blooms. But feeding now can actually encourage leafy growth at the expense of flowers. Hold off on fertilizer until early spring, when the plant is gearing up for the growing season.

Similarly, don't divide agapanthus in August. Division is best done in spring or autumn when the plant is dormant. Disturbing the roots during active growth can stress the plant and reduce flowering. Patience is key—let your agapanthus enjoy its summer rest.
