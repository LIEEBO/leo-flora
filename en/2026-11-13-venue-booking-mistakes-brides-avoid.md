## 5 Venue Booking Mistakes Brides Make (And How to Avoid Them)

You've found the perfect venue: the ballroom with soaring ceilings, the garden that glows at golden hour, the coordinator who said yes to everything. It feels like *the one*, and the pressure to put down a deposit before someone else snatches your date is real.

But pause for a moment. The venue contract is one of the first documents you'll sign and one of the hardest to undo. Brides who've been through it will tell you the same thing: the tour shows you the room, but it doesn't tell you how the room will work for *your* wedding.

Here are five things they wish they'd known first—plus practical tips to help you avoid the same pitfalls.

## 1. Capacity Is Not the Same as Comfort

A venue listing may say it holds 500. Ask what that means. Is it 500 seated at rounds, or 500 standing at a cocktail reception? South Asian weddings usually need space for a stage or mandap, a dance floor, a sweetheart table or sangeet performance area, and buffet or station lines—all at once.

**Ask the venue:**

- What is the maximum seated capacity with a dance floor and a mandap?
- Can you show me a floor plan for a wedding my size?
- Does capacity change by function (ceremony, cocktail hour, reception)?

And remember: South Asian guest lists tend to grow. Book for the count you may end up with, not only the count on today's spreadsheet.

## 2. Vendor Rules Can Make or Break Your Vision

Many venues require you to use their in-house caterer, decorator, bar service, or preferred vendor list. That can quietly rule out the family caterer, the florist you've dreamed about, or the Indian menu your parents are counting on.

**Ask the venue:**

- Can we bring in an outside caterer, and is there a fee for doing so?
- Are there restrictions on outside decorators, DJs, photographers, or videographers?
- Is there a kitchen suitable for Indian cooking, or is food prepared off-site and reheated?

If your caterer and decor team are non-negotiable, find that out *before* you fall in love with the room.

## 3. Ask About the Baraat, the Fire, and the Sound

South Asian weddings come with unique logistical needs: a baraat procession, a fire ceremony, and often loud music and dancing. Not every venue is equipped for these.

**Ask the venue:**

- Is there a designated area for a baraat, and what are the noise restrictions?
- Are open flames (candles, havan) allowed? What are the insurance requirements?
- Is there a sound system, or do we need to bring our own? Are there decibel limits?

These details can derail your plans if discovered too late. Get them in writing.

## 4. Don't Overlook Decor Restrictions and Setup Time

Your vision of a grand entrance with a lush **wedding flower wall backdrop** and cascading **faux floral arrangements** might hit a snag if the venue has strict rules about hanging things, using nails, or bringing in outside decor.

**Ask the venue:**

- What are the rules for hanging decor (tape, nails, rigging points)?
- How many hours do we have for setup and tear-down? Is there a fee for extra time?
- Is there storage space for decor items before and after the event?

If you're planning a DIY approach with **wholesale silk flowers**, you'll want ample time to set up. Many venues offer limited access, so confirm the schedule early.

## 5. Get Everything in Writing (Including the Little Things)

Verbal promises from the coordinator mean nothing if they're not in the contract. Brides often regret not getting details like parking, accessibility, and contingency plans in writing.

**Ask the venue:**

- What is the cancellation policy and force majeure clause?
- Is there a rain plan (for outdoor spaces)?
- What are the parking and accessibility arrangements for guests?
