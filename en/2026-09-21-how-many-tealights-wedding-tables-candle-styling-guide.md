# How Many Tealights Do You Need for Wedding Tables? Candle Styling Made Simple

There's nothing quite like the warm glow of candlelight at a wedding. It flatters your flowers, your guests — even your food looks better under that soft flicker. But when it comes to tealights, the question every couple asks is: **how many do we actually need?**

Use too few, and your tablescape can feel a little flat. Use too many, and you're burning through your budget (and worrying about fire safety all night). The good news? There's a simple formula that takes the guesswork out of candle styling — and it pairs beautifully with artificial blooms.

## The Simple Tealight Rule: 3 to 5 Per Guest Table

For a standard round table seating 8 to 10 guests, plan on **3 to 5 tealights per table** as a baseline. This gives you a warm, even glow without overwhelming the space.

Here's how to fine-tune that number:

- **Small tables (6 guests or fewer):** 2 to 3 tealights
- **Standard round tables (8–10 guests):** 3 to 5 tealights
- **Long banquet tables:** 1 tealight every 18–24 inches along the center
- **Head table or sweetheart table:** 4 to 6 tealights for a focal glow

If your venue has high ceilings or lots of ambient light, lean toward the higher end. Intimate, low-lit spaces? Stick with the lower count — candlelight reads brighter in the dark.

## Layer Your Lighting for Maximum Impact

Tealights alone are lovely, but the real magic happens when you **layer different light sources**. Think of it like styling an outfit: one piece is nice, but the combination is what turns heads.

Try this trio:

1. **Tealights** for that low, romantic base glow
2. **Taper or pillar candles** in holders for height and drama
3. **Fairy lights or votives** woven through your centerpiece for sparkle

This layered approach means you can actually use *fewer* tealights while creating a richer effect. It's a budget-friendly trick that wedding planners swear by.

## Pairing Candles With Faux Florals (The Smart Move)

Here's where candle styling gets really practical. Real flowers and open flames don't always mix well — petals wilt, leaves dry out, and you're constantly worried about a stray spark. That's why so many couples now build their tablescapes around **artificial flowers** instead.

A **fake flowers centerpiece** gives you three big advantages:

- **Fire-safe placement:** You can tuck tealights right into the arrangement without worrying about heat damage.
- **All-day setup:** Faux blooms look identical at noon and midnight, so you can style tables the day before.
- **Zero wilting:** Your centerpieces stay flawless from first guest to last dance.

For a cohesive look, match your candle holders to your floral palette — gold or brass for warm tones, matte black or glass for modern schemes.

## Practical Safety and Styling Tips

Candles and celebrations go hand in hand, but a little planning keeps everything safe and stress-free:

- **Use LED tealights** at venues that ban open flames — they look surprisingly realistic now.
- **Place tealights in glass holders** to protect tables and contain wax.
- **Keep flames away from fabric runners** and low-hanging décor.
- **Assign someone** to do a quick candle check before the reception begins.
- **Cluster in odd numbers** (3, 5, 7) — it's a designer trick that looks more organic than even groupings.
