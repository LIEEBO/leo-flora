## Introduction

Picture this: a mandap facing the ocean, a baraat rolling up a palm-lined drive, fireworks lighting up the sky over Los Cabos. Or a sleek ballroom in Mexico City, with family flying in from three continents for a weekend they'll talk about for years.

Then the doubts creep in. An aunt worries nobody will make the trip. A friend says resorts won't allow a sacred fire. Someone insists it will cost twice as much as a wedding at home.

Some concerns are worth planning around. Most are myths. Here's what couples who have married in Mexico have learned—and what it really takes to bring a South Asian celebration to life there.

## Myth 1: "Guests won't travel that far."

**Reality:** For many families, a destination wedding is the reason to travel. South Asian families are often spread across the US, Canada, the UK, and India, so many guests are flying to the wedding no matter where it is. Mexico is one of the easiest places to bring them together.

Los Cabos and Mexico City both have direct flights from many major US and Canadian cities, and they're in time zones close to North America's. Instead of one long evening, guests get a long weekend with sun, great food, and the people they love.

**What to plan for:**

- **Send save-the-dates early.** Give guests 8 to 12 months so they can request time off, renew passports, and find good fares.
- **Reserve a hotel room block.** Booking guests at or near your venue makes the weekend feel like a single shared celebration.
- **Make a simple travel page.** List the nearest airport, transfer options, and the dress code for each event.
- **Think about older relatives.** Ask the venue about accessible rooms, shuttle service, and shaded seating.

## Myth 2: "Local venues won't understand our traditions."

**Reality:** Mexico's top hotels and resorts host weddings from all over the world, and many have now welcomed South Asian couples. The best ones don't treat a Mehndi, a Sangeet, or a Vidaai as an unusual request. They plan for these events from the very first site visit.

The key is choosing a venue with real experience, not just enthusiasm. A venue that has done it before already knows how much floor space a mandap and a dance floor need. It knows how to turn a ceremony room into a reception and how to work with an outside Indian caterer or a chef who can cook for vegetarian guests.

**What to plan for:**

- **Ask for proof.** Request references from past South Asian weddings and ask detailed questions about logistics.
- **Visit in person.** A site visit lets you see the spaces and discuss setup for each event.
- **Clarify vendor policies.** Confirm whether outside vendors (caterers, decorators, priests) are allowed and what fees apply.

## Practical Tips for a Smooth Destination Wedding

- **Hire a local wedding planner.** They know the vendors, language, and logistics.
- **Create a detailed timeline.** Share it with vendors and guests to keep everyone on track.
- **Prepare a backup plan.** Weather, travel delays, and other surprises can happen—flexibility is key.
- **Communicate clearly.** Use a wedding website or app to share updates and details.
