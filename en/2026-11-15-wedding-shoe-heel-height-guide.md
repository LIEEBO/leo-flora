## Why Heel Height Matters More Than You Think

The height of your heels isn't just about fashion—it's about function. A higher heel changes your posture, alters the drape of your gown, and can make or break your comfort over a long day. Low heels or flats offer stability and are ideal for outdoor venues, while mid-heels provide a gentle lift without sacrificing too much comfort. High heels add drama and elegance but require practice and the right terrain. Consider your venue: grassy gardens, sandy beaches, and historic cobblestones all demand careful shoe choices. And don't forget the hem of your dress—it should be tailored to your chosen heel height to avoid tripping or dragging.

## Practical Tips for Choosing Your Perfect Heel Height

1. **Match the heel to your venue.** For outdoor ceremonies, opt for block heels or wedges that won't sink into grass or gravel. Indoor ballrooms allow for stilettos or kitten heels.
2. **Consider your dress length.** If you're wearing a floor-length gown, have it hemmed while wearing the shoes you'll actually walk down the aisle in. A difference of even half an inch can affect the fit.
3. **Test-drive your shoes.** Wear them around the house for a few days before the wedding. This helps you break them in and identify any pressure points.
4. **Bring a backup pair.** Many brides switch to flats or sandals for the reception. This is especially wise if you've chosen high heels.
5. **Think about your partner's height.** If you're significantly taller than your partner in heels, you might prefer a lower heel for photos and comfort.
