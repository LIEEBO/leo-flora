# May Birth Flowers: Lily of the Valley & Hawthorn for Weddings

May is one of those rare months that gets two birth flowers, and they could not be more different. One is a tiny, almost shy white bloom that has found its way into more royal wedding bouquets than nearly any other spring flower. The other is a thorned tree so sacred in Celtic folklore that Ireland once rerouted a motorway rather than cut one down.

For DIY brides and wedding planners, understanding these two May birth flowers can shape everything from your bouquet to your wedding flower wall backdrop. Let's walk through both, because once you understand them, choosing for a May birthday or a May wedding becomes easy.

## Why Does May Have Two Birth Flowers?

May ended up with two because different regions grew different blooms in different flowering seasons, and the tradition simply kept both. Lily of the valley is a shade-loving ground cover. Hawthorn is a blossoming tree native to three continents. They share almost nothing, and that is exactly what makes the pairing so lovely to design with.

One bloom is petite and perfumed, barely 12 inches tall. The other climbs 15 to 30 feet into the air. When a client asks about a May birthday arrangement, I usually ask which one sounds more like the person they are celebrating. That single question narrows the whole thing down in seconds.

## May Birth Flower Number One: Lily of the Valley

### A Spring Bloom With a Misleading Name

Despite the name, lily of the valley (Convallaria majalis) belongs to the asparagus family, not the lily family at all. Its Latin name translates to "belonging to May," which feels exactly right. Each plant sends up arching stems lined with tiny white bells that top out around 6 to 12 inches. You get a short, precious window, only two to four weeks of blooms each spring before it closes again.

Older names for it include Our Lady's Tears and May Bells, and the French call it Muguet. Left alone in a garden, it spreads through its roots faster than anyone expects.

### The Stories Behind It

Christian legend says lily of the valley sprang from the Virgin Mary's tears, which is where its symbolism of purity and motherhood comes from. The English version has more drama to it. As the story goes, St. Leonard fought a dragon in a Sussex forest, and lily of the valley bloomed wherever his blood fell. That patch of woodland is still called The Lily Beds, and the flowers return there every spring.

France turned the bloom into a national tradition called La Fête du Muguet, and to this day sprigs of lily of the valley are sold all over the country on the first of May.

### The Most Toxic Flower in a Wedding Bouquet

Here's the catch: lily of the valley is highly toxic. Every part of the plant, including the water in the vase, contains cardiac glycosides. If you're planning a wedding with children or pets present, this is a serious consideration. Many florists now recommend artificial lily of the valley for bouquets and boutonnieres, especially for outdoor ceremonies where little hands might wander. Artificial flowers for wedding use give you the same delicate look without the risk.

## May Birth Flower Number Two: Hawthorn

### A Tree With Deep Roots in Folklore

Hawthorn (Crataegus) is a thorny tree or shrub that bursts into clusters of white or pink blossoms in May. In Celtic tradition, it is considered sacred and associated with fairies and the spirit world. The Irish went so far as to reroute a motorway to avoid cutting down a lone hawthorn tree. That's how much this plant matters in folklore.

In wedding symbolism, hawthorn represents hope and protection. Its branches add a wild, romantic texture that few other spring blooms can match. I love using hawthorn branches in larger installations, especially when I want a little untamed beauty.

### Designing With Hawthorn

Hawthorn works beautifully in:

- **Ceremony arches and chuppahs** – the thorny branches create a natural, organic frame
- **Table centerpieces** – paired with softer blooms like roses or peonies
- **Wedding flower wall backdrop** – the texture adds depth and movement

Because hawthorn blooms are short-lived, many planners turn to high-quality artificial branches to achieve the look without the wilt. Artificial flowers for wedding decor have come a long way, and today's faux hawthorn looks remarkably real.

## Practical Tips for Using May Birth Flowers in Weddings

### 1. Mix Real and Artificial

If you love the look of lily of the valley but worry about toxicity or cost, consider a mix. Use real blooms in the bridal bouquet and artificial lily of the valley for bridesmaids, boutonnieres, and table arrangements. This keeps costs down and eliminates safety concerns.

### 2. Plan Around the Short Season

Both lily of the valley and hawthorn have brief blooming windows. If your wedding falls outside those weeks, artificial flowers are your best friend. They give you the exact look you want, any time of year, without the premium price tag.

### 3. Choose Affordable Wedding Flowers Wisely

Lily of the valley is one of the most expensive flowers per stem, often costing $5 to $10 each. For a full bouquet, that adds up fast. ### 4. Consider the Venue
