# Floating Candle Centrepiece: A Complete DIY Guide for Weddings

A floating candle centrepiece is one of the most affordable ways to make a long table look elegant and finished. The concept is simple: a glass vessel, clean water, a few floating candles, and something beautiful underneath. Done well, it reads as calm and expensive; done badly, it means wax on the linen, candles drifting into a clump, and a venue manager asking you to blow everything out before the speeches.

This guide covers everything you need to know—materials, a step-by-step build, realistic costs, and the safety rules most venues will hold you to. Plus, we'll share how to incorporate **silk flowers wedding decor** and **wedding venue decoration ideas** to elevate your centrepieces.

## Why a Floating Candle Centrepiece Works

Floating candles sit low, so guests can see and talk across the table. The water multiplies the light, meaning fewer candles give a stronger glow than the same number of pillar candles. The look also adapts to almost any palette: clear glass and white candles for a classic wedding, smoked glass and amber tones for an autumn dinner, or a few olive leaves under the water for a green-themed celebration.

The other advantage is preparation time. Once the vessels are clean and the water is measured, each centrepiece takes two or three minutes to assemble on site. For a 15-table wedding, that's a job one person can finish in under an hour.

## Materials for One Floating Candle Centrepiece

- **Glass vessel:** a cylinder 15–25 cm wide and 20–40 cm tall, or a low bowl 25–30 cm wide. Straight sides look cleaner and are easier to light.
- **Floating candles:** unscented, 5–8 cm wide. Plan on a burn time of at least five hours so they last from the drinks reception to the last dance. Unscented matters—scented candles compete with the food.
- **Clean water:** filtered or tap water left to stand, so you avoid bubbles clinging to the glass.
- **Something underneath:** river stones, glass pebbles, sliced citrus, cranberries, eucalyptus sprigs, or a single orchid stem. Keep organic material fully submerged so it doesn't touch the flame.
- **A long lighter** and a small jug for topping up water.
- **A tray or charger plate** under the vessel to protect the linen and catch any drips.

## Step-by-Step: Building the Centrepiece

1. **Clean the glass properly.** Wash, dry, and polish with a lint-free cloth. Fingerprints and water spots are the first thing guests notice when a candle is lit behind them.
2. **Add the base layer.** Place stones or fruit first, then any greenery. If you use flowers, weigh the stem down with a stone so it stays under the surface.
3. **Pour the water slowly.** Fill to about 5 cm below the rim. The gap protects the flame from draughts and stops splashes when someone knocks the table.
4. **Float the candles.** One candle per 8–10 cm of surface width is a good rule. For a 20 cm wide cylinder, use two candles.
5. **Light and adjust.** Use a long lighter, and keep a spare candle nearby in case one sinks or goes out.

## Venue Safety Rules You Must Follow

Most venues have strict fire safety policies. Always check with your venue before lighting any candles. Common rules include:

- Candles must be enclosed in glass (like your vessel) or be floating.
- Flames must be at least 30 cm away from anything flammable, including floral arrangements.
- You may need to extinguish candles before speeches or when guests leave.
- Some venues require battery-operated candles instead of real flames.

To be safe, consider using LED floating candles—they look just as lovely and eliminate fire risk.

## Practical Tips for a Flawless Setup

- **Test ahead:** Assemble one centrepiece at home to check burn time and water clarity.
- **Use distilled water:** It stays clearer longer and reduces mineral deposits.
- **Keep it simple:** One type of base material (e.g., all white stones) looks more elegant than a mix.
- **Add height:** Pair low floating centrepieces with taller **silk flowers wedding decor** arrangements for visual interest.
- **Label boxes:** Pack each centrepiece's components in separate boxes for easy setup.
