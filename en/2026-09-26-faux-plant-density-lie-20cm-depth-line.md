# The Density Lie: Why Your Faux Plant Reads as Plastic & The 20 cm Depth Line That Fixes It

You've spent weeks curating wedding venue decoration ideas. The arch is draped, the tables are set, and your faux floral arrangements look... fine. But in photos? They read flat, plastic, and obviously fake. The culprit isn't the leaves or the pot — it's density. Most faux plants fail because their canopy is one even, packed mass from every angle. Real plants never grow that way. They grow in layers, with gaps and shadows. And there's a simple fix: the 20 cm depth line.

## Why Uniform Density Screams "Fake"

A real plant's canopy is a conversation between light and shadow. Leaves overlap, twist, and create pockets of darkness. The eye reads those gaps as depth and life. When every leaf sits at the same distance from the stem, the brain instantly flags it as artificial — even if the individual leaves are high-quality silk.

This is why so many silk flowers wedding decor setups look beautiful in person but disappointing in photos. The camera flattens an already-flat arrangement. The fix isn't more flowers; it's smarter spacing.

## The 20 cm Depth Line: What It Is and How to Use It

The 20 cm depth line is a simple rule: no leaf should sit directly behind another leaf within 20 cm (about 8 inches) without a visible gap or offset. In practice, this means:

- **Stagger heights.** Pull some stems forward, push others back. Create a front, middle, and back layer.
- **Leave negative space.** A 2–3 cm gap between leaf clusters lets shadow do the work.
- **Angle leaves outward.** Real leaves turn toward light. Rotate yours slightly so they don't all face the same way.
- **Vary density.** Pack the center, loosen the edges. Real growth is never uniform.

This single adjustment transforms faux floral arrangements from "obviously fake" to "wait, is that real?"

## Practical Tips for DIY Brides and Planners

### 1. Build in Layers, Not Clumps

When assembling a centerpiece or arch, think in three depths: front (closest to viewer), middle (the bulk), and back (the backdrop). Use taller stems in the back, mid-height in the middle, and trailing or low leaves in front. This creates a natural 20 cm depth rhythm.

### 2. Mix Textures and Shades

Uniform color and texture flatten an arrangement. Combine matte and glossy leaves, deep greens with lighter greens, and a few dried or brown-toned elements. This mimics how real plants age and catch light differently.

### 3. Trust the Gaps

Beginners tend to fill every hole. Resist. A 2–3 cm gap between clusters reads as intentional and organic. Your wedding venue decoration ideas will look more editorial and less craft-store.

### 4. Fluff and Bend

Most faux stems arrive compressed. Before arranging, gently bend stems and fluff leaves outward. This alone can add 30% more perceived depth.

### 5. Source Stems That Support Depth

Not all faux flowers are built for layering. Look for stems with wired, bendable cores and leaves that can be rotated independently. Artificial flowers from leofaux are designed with this flexibility, making it easier to build the 20 cm depth line into every arrangement.

## Why This Matters for Weddings

Wedding photos are unforgiving. Flat arrangements look cheap in bright daylight and washed out under reception lighting. By applying the 20 cm depth line, your silk flowers wedding decor will hold up in every shot — from the ceremony arch to the centerpieces.

Planners who master this technique can also reuse arrangements across multiple events without them looking stale, because the depth keeps them visually interesting from every angle.

## Conclusion

The density lie is simple: more flowers don't make an arrangement look real. Depth does. By keeping leaves at least 20 cm apart in depth, staggering heights, and embracing gaps, your faux floral arrangements will read as lush and living — not plastic.
