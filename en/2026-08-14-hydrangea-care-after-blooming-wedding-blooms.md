# Hydrangea Care After Blooming for Stunning Wedding Blooms

If you're dreaming of lush hydrangea blooms for your wedding—whether in your bridal bouquet or as part of your wedding venue decoration ideas—you might be growing them yourself to save on costs. But did you know that how you care for your hydrangeas after they finish flowering can make or break next year's display? Pruning at the wrong time could mean fewer flowers, and that's the last thing you want when you're planning your big day.

In this guide, we'll walk you through the dos and don'ts of hydrangea pruning after blooming, so you can enjoy a stunning floral show year after year. Plus, we'll share some affordable wedding flowers and fake flowers centerpiece ideas that will keep your decor budget-friendly without sacrificing beauty.

## Know Your Hydrangea Type: Old Wood vs. New Wood

Hydrangeas fall into two main categories: those that bloom on old wood and those that bloom on new wood. The old-wood varieties are the ones you need to be careful with. These include:

- **Bigleaf hydrangeas** (_Hydrangea macrophylla_) – known for their large, rounded mophead blooms or delicate lacecap flowers.
- **Oakleaf hydrangeas** (_Hydrangea quercifolia_) – easily recognized by their oak-shaped leaves and cone-shaped flower clusters.
- **Mountain hydrangeas** (_Hydrangea serrata_) – similar to bigleaf but often with smaller, lacecap-style blooms.

These hydrangeas form next year's flower buds in late summer and fall. So if you prune them too late, you risk cutting off the buds that would have become your gorgeous wedding flowers next spring.

## When to Prune Old-Wood Hydrangeas

The safest time to prune old-wood hydrangeas is **shortly after they finish flowering**—typically in mid-summer. This gives the plant plenty of time to develop new buds before the next season. If you missed that window, it's better to prune less or not at all. A conservative approach will help protect next year's blooms.

### Tips for Pruning:
- **Remove only dead or damaged wood** if you're unsure about timing.
- **Shape lightly** to maintain size, but avoid heavy cutting.
- **Skip pruning altogether** if the plant is healthy and the size is right.

## What About Reblooming Varieties?

Some newer bigleaf and mountain hydrangeas are reblooming types. These can flower on both old and new wood, which means they're more forgiving if you prune at the wrong time. If you have these, you have a little more flexibility. Still, it's best to follow the same general rule: prune after flowering to encourage a strong bloom cycle.

## Hydrangea Care Beyond Pruning

Pruning isn't the only thing that affects next year's blooms. Here are a few extra tips to keep your hydrangeas thriving:

- **Water deeply** during dry spells, especially in late summer when buds are forming.
- **Mulch around the base** to retain moisture and regulate soil temperature.
- **Avoid high-nitrogen fertilizers** in late summer; opt for a phosphorus-rich feed to encourage bud development.

## Bringing Hydrangeas into Your Wedding Decor

Even if you don't have a green thumb, you can still enjoy hydrangeas in your wedding decor. For affordable wedding flowers, consider mixing fresh hydrangeas with greenery and filler blooms. They're relatively inexpensive and make a big impact.

If you're planning ahead or want decor that lasts beyond the wedding day, artificial hydrangeas are a fantastic option. High-quality silk hydrangeas look remarkably real and can be used in **fake flowers centerpiece** arrangements that you can keep as keepsakes or repurpose for other events. Plus, they're perfect for wedding venue decoration ideas where fresh flowers might wilt in warm weather.

### DIY Centerpiece Idea:
Create a stunning fake flowers centerpiece by pairing artificial hydrangeas with eucalyptus stems and candles in mason jars. It's budget-friendly, easy to assemble, and looks elegant at any reception table.

## Conclusion

Caring for your hydrangeas after flowering is all about timing. Know your plant type, prune early if needed, and when in doubt, leave them be. Whether you're growing fresh blooms for your wedding or opting for realistic artificial alternatives, hydrangeas add timeless beauty to any celebration. And with these tips, you'll have gorgeous flowers—real or faux—for your big day and beyond.
