# The Broom Lie: Why Artificial Flowers in a Vase Look Planted, and the 15-Degree Fan That Holds

If you've ever arranged artificial flowers in a vase and felt disappointed with the result, you're not alone. Most faux floral arrangements fail in the same predictable way: every stem sits at the same angle, every flower head reaches the same height, and the whole thing looks like a broom stuck in a vase. We've seen this happen in hotel lobbies, retail windows, and even wedding tables. The good news? The fix isn't more stems—it's a simple technique called the 15-degree fan.

In this guide, we'll explain why the broom effect happens and show you exactly how to arrange realistic artificial flowers so they look naturally planted, not stuffed. Whether you're a DIY bride or a professional wedding planner, these tips will help you create stunning arrangements that rival fresh flowers—without the wilt and the hefty price tag.

## Why the Broom Effect Happens

The broom effect occurs when stems are arranged too uniformly. When every stem is inserted straight up and down, the flowers cluster together at the top, creating a dense, flat-topped shape that resembles a broom. This happens because most people naturally grab a bunch of stems and push them into the vase without separating or angling them. The result is a stiff, unnatural look that screams "artificial."

Real flowers in nature don't grow in perfect unison. They reach for light, bend with the wind, and have varying heights and angles. To mimic that organic feel, you need to introduce variation—and that's where the 15-degree fan comes in.

## The 15-Degree Fan Technique

The 15-degree fan is a simple yet powerful method for arranging artificial flowers. Instead of placing stems vertically, you angle each stem outward by about 15 degrees from the center. This creates a fan-like spread that gives the arrangement volume, depth, and a natural, garden-gathered look.

Here's how to do it:

1. **Start with a clean vase** and add a base of filler greens or a stem-holding mechanism (like floral foam or a grid of tape).
2. **Begin with the tallest stems** in the center, angling them slightly outward.
3. **Work outward in a circle**, placing each subsequent stem at a 15-degree angle from the previous one.
4. **Vary the heights**—don't let all flower heads sit at the same level. Trim stems to different lengths to create a natural cascade.
5. **Rotate the vase** as you work to ensure even distribution from all sides.

The 15-degree fan not only prevents the broom effect but also allows each bloom to be visible, showcasing the beauty of your realistic artificial flowers.
